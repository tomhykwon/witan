Not a paper session. A discussion.

Witan is Old English for “the wise.” It was a council convened to advise Anglo-Saxon kings on matters of consequence — and, when the moment demanded, to elect them. The last Witan in Oxford, held 961 years ago, set in motion the events of 1066.

Our contemporary Witan is a one-day forum for the UK strategy community to reflect together on the questions strategy scholarship ought to be taking seriously — and what, if anything, our work can do about them. It is not a venue for presenting one’s latest paper.

Hosted by Anil Doshi & Tom Kwon, UCL School of Management

About the Witan

Why the Witan?

01

Talk about the field, not your latest paper

A day set aside for the questions strategy scholarship should be taking seriously, rather than for presenting finished work.

02

Set future agendas together

Parallel tracks on theoretical traditions and on topical areas — from governance and societal issues to technology and AI.

03

Debate, roundtables and half-baked ideas

Formats built for disagreement and new connections, from an Oxford Union–style debate to rotating conversations about early ideas.

04

A record that outlives the day

Notes and dispatches on the blog, so the substance of each Witan is available to the wider community as the event moves between hosts.

What happens on the day

Based on the 2026 programme at Oxford. The 2027 programme will be announced in due course.

  1. MorningOpening panel

    Provocations on how strategy scholarship should change with the world it studies.

  2. Late morningFuture agendas: theory

    Parallel tracks on behavioural strategy, institutional theory, and strategy as practice.

  3. MiddayHalf-baked ideas

    Speed-friending around early-stage ideas.

  4. AfternoonFuture agendas: topics

    Governance, societal issues, and technology and AI.

  5. Late afternoonThe debate

    “This House believes strategy as a field should no longer exist.”

  6. EveningRoundtables & dinner

    Designing the PhD strategy syllabus of the future, then dinner.
